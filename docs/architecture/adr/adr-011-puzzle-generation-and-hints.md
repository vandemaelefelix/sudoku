# ADR-011: On-device generation, graded by a human-technique solver that also gives the hints

| Field   | Value      |
| ------- | ---------- |
| Project | Sudoku     |
| Status  | Proposed   |
| Date    | 2026-10-09 |
| Contact | Felix      |

## Context

The game needs puzzles at several difficulty levels (F-01), and hints for any puzzle, generated
or scanned (F-07). A playable board must appear within 2 s of a cold start (QR-12). Difficulty
for a human depends on which solving techniques a puzzle needs, not on how many givens it has.
A logical hint is the next step one of those techniques finds.

## Alternatives Evaluated

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bundled puzzle bank (a few thousand, pre-graded) | Instant. Grading done offline | Finite. Another dataset with its own licence question. No grader on the phone, so scanned puzzles get no difficulty |
| Generate on demand, grade by number of givens | Simple | Givens count is a poor proxy for difficulty |
| **Generate on the device, grade with a technique solver, keep a small pool** | Unlimited puzzles. One solver grades, hints and rates scanned puzzles. Good learning value | Hard puzzles can take many attempts. Needs the pool to meet QR-12 |

## Decision

1. **`@sudoku/core` holds three solvers with one purpose each:**
   - a fast **backtracking solver** that counts solutions (0, 1, many). It checks uniqueness,
     during generation and for scanned givens,
   - a **technique solver** that solves the way a person does: singles, pairs, pointing, X-wing
     and so on, each technique with a difficulty weight,
   - a **generator**: make a full solution, remove givens while the puzzle stays unique, then
     grade it with the technique solver.
2. **Difficulty** is the hardest technique the technique solver needs, plus a weighted count.
   The levels (easy to expert) are bands on that score. A puzzle that needs guessing is rejected
   by the generator.
3. **Hints** are the technique solver's next step on the player's current board. A hint first
   names the area ("look at row 4"), then the technique, then the cell. If no technique applies,
   the fallback reveals one cell from the solution, and says so.
4. **Pool.** The app keeps 3 ready puzzles per difficulty in local storage (ADR-012). Starting a
   game takes one from the pool. Refilling runs in the background off the JS thread. On first
   launch the pool is seeded from a small bundled set (3 per level, generated by the same code),
   so QR-12 holds from the very first start.
5. **Scanned puzzles** are graded by the same technique solver, so they get a difficulty too.

## Amendment: v1 rules for the level and the hint (2026-10-12)

v1 does not build the technique solver. The game must be playable first. These rules apply
until the technique solver exists:

- **Level by givens.** The generator makes a full solution, then removes givens in a random
  order while the backtracking solver still finds exactly one solution. It stops at the target
  of the level: easy 40, medium 34, hard 29, expert 25 givens.
- **Seed.** A seed and a level name one puzzle. The same seed and level give the same puzzle on
  every device. The random source is mulberry32.
- **Hint.** A hint reveals one random empty or wrong cell (point 3, the fallback). It does not
  name an area or a technique yet.
- **No pool.** Generation takes less than 5 ms per puzzle on a Mac (median below 1 ms at every
  level). That is far below the 2 s of QR-12, so v1 has no pool (point 4). The pool returns when
  a level takes more than 1 s.
- **Scanned puzzles** get no level in v1 (point 5).
- Grading by technique comes back with #53. Hints that teach come back with #45.
- Consequence: two puzzles at the same level can differ a lot in difficulty, because the number
  of givens is a poor measure (see Context). v1 accepts this.

## Amendment: a level for scanned puzzles (2026-10-12)

The game in progress and the history need a level for every game (ADR-012). So a scanned puzzle
gets a level in v1, against the bullet "Scanned puzzles get no level in v1" above.

- A scanned puzzle takes the level whose givens target is nearest to its number of givens. The
  targets are the targets of the generator: easy 40, medium 34, hard 29, expert 25.
- A tie goes to the easier level. More than 40 givens is easy. Fewer than 25 givens is expert.
- The source of a scanned game is `scanned` (ADR-012). The finish card and the history show it.
- The technique solver (#53) replaces this rule. Then a scanned puzzle gets the level of its
  techniques (point 5).
- The amendment "a level for scanned puzzles by technique" (2026-10-21) replaces this rule.

## Amendment: the puzzle number (2026-10-12)

The finish card shows the number of a generated puzzle (#43). The seed is that number.

- The app draws seeds from 0 to 999 999, so the number stays short: "Puzzle 412 077".
  `generate()` still accepts every seed from 0 to 0xffffffff.
- One million puzzles per level is enough for v1. A player meets a puzzle twice only by chance.
- The seed is stored with the game in progress and the history (ADR-012, amendment "the seed").
- A scanned puzzle has no seed. Its card says "Scanned from paper".

## Amendment: generation by technique (2026-10-21)

`generate(level, seed)` keeps only a puzzle whose `grade()` is that level (#202). The number of
givens no longer sets the level. The givens count of a generated puzzle is a result, not a target.
`GIVENS_TARGET` is gone (#203).

- **One stream.** The seed makes one random stream. Each attempt draws a full grid. It then
  removes givens in random order while the puzzle stays unique. The removal stops at the floor of
  the level.
- **Accept rule.** An attempt is accepted when `grade(givens).level` is the wanted level. The
  generator never accepts or returns a `beyond` puzzle. `gradesAs(givens, level)` gives the same
  answer as `grade()`. It runs the solver with the levels up to the wanted level only. It stops
  at the first step of a harder level. It computes the choice only when the hardest technique is
  a single. A miss costs less. A test proves the equality at 40, 34, 29 and 17 givens, and at
  the border of `EASY_MIN_CHOICE`.
- **Floor.** The floor is a number of givens where the removal stops. It keeps an easy puzzle
  open. The grade decides the level, not the floor. The yields come from the research record
  (section 8) and a re-measure of 4 000 seeds per floor. The time per puzzle is the time of one
  attempt (removal and `gradesAs`) divided by the yield. The table shows the floor that the
  generator uses, and for hard and expert the floors that lost:

  | Level | Floor | Yield % | Attempts | Time per puzzle (ms) | Why |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | easy | 40 | 53 | 1.9 | 0.9 | Only about 40 givens and more give a choice of 19 or more. At 34 givens, 6 % are easy |
  | medium | 34 | 91 | 1.1 | 0.6 | Almost every puzzle with 34 givens is medium. Fewer givens only add `beyond` |
  | hard | 29 | 8.6 | 11.6 | 10.3 | The lowest time per puzzle |
  | hard | 25 | 15.3 | 6.5 | 14.7 | The removal costs 1.6 ms per attempt, against 0.4 ms at 29 |
  | hard | 17 | 16.4 | 6.1 | 23.2 | A minimal puzzle. The removal costs 3.1 ms per attempt |
  | expert | 25 | 7.8 | 12.8 | 31.9 | The same time as 29, with 2.4 times fewer attempts. The tail is shorter |
  | expert | 29 | 3.3 | 30.3 | 31.5 | |
  | expert | 17 | 8.8 | 11.4 | 46.3 | A minimal puzzle. The removal costs 3.1 ms per attempt |

  The removal is the larger part of an attempt at a low floor. A lower floor raises the yield of
  hard and expert a little, but the removal costs more than it gains. A puzzle of hard or expert
  has more than the floor of givens when the removal cannot reach it. It is still a valid puzzle
  of its level.
- **`MAX_ATTEMPTS` is 250.** Expert is the rarest level. Its yield at 25 givens is 7.8 %, so an
  attempt misses with a chance of 0.922. The chance that all 250 attempts miss is 0.922^250, about
  1.5 in 1 billion. The app draws from 1 000 000 seeds, so about 0.0015 seeds are
  expected to need the fallback. The mean is 12.8 attempts. The p99 is 57 attempts (ln 0.01 / ln 0.922). A miss of all
  attempts costs about 0.6 s on a Mac.
- **Fallback.** When no attempt matches, the generator grades the attempts. It returns the one
  that `fallbackRank` ranks first: the nearest level. At the same distance, a level below the
  wanted level wins, because a puzzle that is too easy is safer than one that is too hard. The
  result is never `beyond`. When every attempt is `beyond`, the generator reveals givens of the
  solution until the solver finishes. A full grid is always easy. The seed stays the same. The
  result is still the same on every device. `generateAtLevel` takes the limit and the accept
  rule as parameters, so a test forces the fallback.
- **Speed of the removal.** The removal checks only if another solution exists with the removed
  cell set to another digit. It starts from the known solution and allocates nothing per call. A
  test shows that it removes the same givens as a removal that counts solutions with `solve()`.
- **Time on a Mac** (Node, one thread, `generate`, 20 seeds in `generator.test.ts`, and 2 000
  seeds for the tail of hard and expert):

  | Level | Median of 20 (ms) | Slowest of 20 (ms) | Median of 2 000 (ms) | p99 of 2 000 (ms) | Slowest of 2 000 (ms) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | easy | 0.5 | 2.3 | - | - | - |
  | medium | 0.6 | 1.1 | - | - | - |
  | hard | 9.6 | 42 | 7.8 | 48 | 88 |
  | expert | 23.5 | 94 | 23.0 | 133 | 243 |

  The tail of easy and medium is not measured. It is far below hard. One expert attempt takes
  2.5 ms: 1.6 ms for the removal and 0.9 ms for `gradesAs`. The removal is about two thirds of
  the time, so the solver is not the bottleneck and `technique/` stays as it is. The test bounds
  the median, not the slowest seed, so a busy CI worker does not fail it.
- **Estimate for an iPhone.** Hermes is 5 to 10 times slower than Node on a Mac. This is an
  assumption, not a measurement. Expert then takes 115 to 230 ms at the median. It takes 0.7 to
  1.3 s at p99 and 1.2 to 2.4 s at the slowest of 2 000. Hard takes 0.2 to 0.5 s at p99.
- **Pool.** The rule of point 4 is that the pool returns when a level takes more than 1 s. The
  pool is not needed on a Mac, where no level comes near 1 s. For the iPhone, the decision waits
  for the iPhone check of epic #204. On the estimate, the slowest expert puzzles can pass 1 s. If
  they pass 1 s in the check, a follow-up issue brings the pool for expert, or a bundled set.
- **History.** A seed now gives another puzzle than before. The history keeps the seed number
  and the level of a saved game. A game in progress keeps its own givens.

## Amendment: a level for scanned puzzles by technique (2026-10-21)

This replaces the amendment "a level for scanned puzzles" (2026-10-12). The rule of that
amendment, the nearest givens target, no longer applies. A scanned puzzle gets the level of its
grade, the same rule as a generated puzzle (point 5).

- A scanned puzzle that passes the play gate has exactly one solution. The app calls `grade()` on
  its givens. The level of the grade is the level of the game.
- A puzzle that the solver does not finish grades as `beyond`. The app maps `beyond` to expert.
  The four levels of the sync contract do not change (ADR-012).
- The correction screen shows no level while the play gate blocks the puzzle. A grade has no
  meaning without a unique solution.
- `scannedLevel` in the app calls `grade()` only for a puzzle that the play gate lets through.
- The source of a scanned game stays `scanned` (ADR-012).

## Consequences

- The technique set grows over time. Each new technique makes hints better and grading finer,
  without any change outside `@sudoku/core`.
- Generation time for expert puzzles is measured in Vitest. Grading must be deterministic for a
  given seed, so tests can pin examples.

## Reassessment Triggers

- Expert generation regularly takes longer than the pool can absorb. Bundle a larger pre-graded
  expert set generated by the same code.
- The backend arrives with a daily puzzle shared by all players. The server generates it with the
  same package.
- Players find that the levels of v1 do not match the difficulty they feel. Bring grading by
  technique forward.

## Amendment 2026-10-21: hints that teach

[ADR-032](adr-032-hints-that-teach.md) supersedes point 3 and the hint bullet of the v1 amendment.

- A hint now teaches in four rungs: look, technique, why and answer.
- The fallback reveals the empty cell with the fewest candidates. It still reveals one cell of the solution.
- The v1 random hint stays until the hint card ships (#196).

## Amendment: grading by technique (2026-10-21)

The technique solver exists (#199, #200, #201, ADR-032). `grade()` in `@sudoku/core` grades a puzzle
with it. This replaces the level-by-givens rule of the amendment of 2026-10-12 for grading. It also
replaces "bands on that score" in point 2. The generator (#202) and the scanned puzzles (#203)
switch to `grade()` in their own issues. Until then the app still sets the level from the givens.

- **Technique level.** Each technique has one level. `TECHNIQUE_LEVEL` holds it. The level of a
  puzzle is the level of the hardest technique that the solve uses. The word "tier" in ADR-032
  names a group of techniques in the hint rollout. It is not a level.

  | Level | Techniques |
  | --- | --- |
  | easy | full house, hidden single, naked single |
  | medium | pointing, box-line reduction, naked and hidden pair, naked and hidden triple |
  | hard | X-wing, swordfish, jellyfish, skyscraper, two-string kite, XY-wing, XYZ-wing, W-wing, naked and hidden quad |
  | expert | unique rectangle, simple colouring, X-chain, XY-chain |

- **Grade order.** The grade runs the solver with `GRADE_ORDER`. It holds the ranks of
  `TECHNIQUE_ORDER` sorted by level: easy, medium, hard, expert. Inside a level the order of
  `TECHNIQUE_ORDER` stays. The reason: in `TECHNIQUE_ORDER` the X-wing comes before the hidden
  pair and the hidden triple. A solve in that order could use an X-wing although medium
  techniques solve the puzzle, and the puzzle would grade hard. Hints keep `TECHNIQUE_ORDER`.
- **Hardest technique.** It is the technique of the highest level among the steps. A tie goes to
  the higher weight.
- **Weight and score.** The weight of a step is its Sudoku Explainer rating in `TECHNIQUE_ORDER`
  (`stepWeight`). The score is the sum of the weights of all steps. For example, a hidden single
  in a box weighs 1.2, a naked single 2.3, pointing 2.6, X-wing 3.2 and XY-chain 6.6. The score
  does not set the level.

  The weights are the Sudoku Explainer ratings in `TECHNIQUE_ORDER`. The table lists the 24 ranks
  of the 23 techniques. The hidden single has one rank for a box and one for a row or a column.

  | Technique | Technique level | Weight |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Full house | easy | 1.0 |
  | Hidden single (box) | easy | 1.2 |
  | Hidden single (row or column) | easy | 1.5 |
  | Naked single | easy | 2.3 |
  | Pointing | medium | 2.6 |
  | Box-line reduction | medium | 2.8 |
  | Naked pair | medium | 3.0 |
  | X-wing | hard | 3.2 |
  | Hidden pair | medium | 3.4 |
  | Naked triple | medium | 3.6 |
  | Swordfish | hard | 3.8 |
  | Hidden triple | medium | 4.0 |
  | Skyscraper | hard | 4.0 |
  | Two-string kite | hard | 4.1 |
  | XY-wing | hard | 4.2 |
  | XYZ-wing | hard | 4.4 |
  | W-wing | hard | 4.4 |
  | Unique rectangle | expert | 4.5 |
  | Simple colouring | expert | 4.6 |
  | Naked quad | hard | 5.0 |
  | Jellyfish | hard | 5.2 |
  | Hidden quad | hard | 5.4 |
  | X-chain | expert | 6.5 |
  | XY-chain | expert | 6.6 |

- **Choice.** The solve runs in rounds. A round fills every empty cell that a full house, a
  hidden single or a naked single can fill, all at once. It counts one move per cell, and each
  move has the number of cells of its round as its value. When no single applies, the round
  applies the first other step in `GRADE_ORDER` and counts one move with the value 0. The choice
  is the mean value of the first 25 moves (`CHOICE_MOVES`). This follows the dependency metric of
  Pelanek. A low choice means that the next cell is hard to find. The rounds do not depend on the
  scan order, so the choice of a puzzle that needs only singles is the same under every symmetry
  transform.
- **Easy threshold.** A puzzle that needs only singles, with a choice below `EASY_MIN_CHOICE`,
  is medium. `EASY_MIN_CHOICE` is 19. At 40 givens, 53 % of the puzzles reach it. At 25 givens
  and for minimal puzzles, none does.
- **`beyond`.** A puzzle that the solver does not finish grades as `beyond`. `beyond` is not a
  level of the game. 9.5 % of the puzzles with 25 givens are `beyond`. The generator rejects
  them.
- **Precondition.** The givens must have exactly one solution. The solver then uses the
  techniques that need a unique solution (unique rectangle). `grade()` does not check this.
- **Stability.** The level does not depend on the orientation or the digit labels of a puzzle.
  In 35 000 measured pairs, the level and the hardest technique never changed.
- **Data.** The research record
  [2026-10-21-technique-grading.md](../../research/2026-10-21-technique-grading.md) measures
  10 000 puzzles. It gives the level shares, the choice, the grade time (10 ms at most on a Mac)
  and the yield for the generator.
- Hints that teach (#45) and the pool of point 4 stay as they were.
